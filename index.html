About me

I build AI systems that have to work outside the lab: segmentation models for biomedical images, computer vision for environmental monitoring, and LLM pipelines that handle sensitive data in healthcare and insurance.

I completed my MSc in Artificial Intelligence at Queen’s University Belfast in 2025, where my dissertation benchmarked Cellpose and the Segment Anything Model on microscopy datasets. Before that I studied Electronics & Telecommunication Engineering at Mumbai University, where I published two papers on deep learning for fish disease detection.

Since graduating I have worked as an AI and MLOps engineer in digital health and insurance technology, contributed to an Alan Turing Institute Data Study Group on detecting oil slicks from shipwrecks in satellite imagery, and led an AI bias audit of job postings with Women in AI Ireland. Across all of it, the question I keep returning to is when can we trust a model on data it wasn’t built for? I want to pursue that question properly in a PhD.

Research interests

The questions I would like to work on during a PhD:

  • How well do foundation segmentation models generalise to biomedical and scientific images they were never trained on? Building on my MSc work comparing Cellpose and SAM across microscopy datasets.
  • How do we detect rare, small targets reliably in noisy imagery, from microscopy to satellite radar? Grounded in small-object detection with YOLO and SAM, and SAR-based oil slick detection with the Turing Institute.
  • How can we evaluate and audit AI systems for bias and failure before they reach people? Drawing on LLM evaluation work, hallucination checks in production, and a fairness audit of job postings.
